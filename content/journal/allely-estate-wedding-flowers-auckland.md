Claire and Dan’s garden wedding at Allely Estate featured sculptural Nursia anthuriums, cascading orchids, flowing bear grass and meaningful soft pink Pastini gerberas. Their organic ceremony arrangement was later repurposed in front of the sweetheart table, creating two distinct floral moments from one thoughtful design.

![An Organic and Sculptural Garden Wedding at Allely Estate, Auckland](/images/an-organic-and-sculptural-garden-wedding-at-allely-estate-auckland)

Claire and Dan’s wedding at Allely Estate is a beautiful example of how thoughtful floral design can create a memorable result without needing flowers in every corner of the venue.

Claire wanted the flowers to feel organic, almost as though they were growing naturally from the ground, while still having a sculptural and editorial edge. With a considered floral budget, we focused on a few carefully planned moments and selected every flower and textural element with intention.

The final design felt soft, modern and a little unexpected, while still sitting naturally within the garden surroundings at Allely Estate.

## An organic ceremony setting beneath the trees

Claire and Dan’s ceremony took place beneath one of Allely Estate’s beautiful mature trees. A simple wooden cross and softly draped ivory fabric created an intimate backdrop, surrounded by the established greenery of the garden.

Rather than adding a traditional floral arch, we designed a low ground arrangement at the base of the cross. Claire wanted it to look as though the flowers had grown there naturally, so the arrangement needed to feel connected to the lawn rather than simply placed on top of it.

![Organic ground ceremony flowers beneath a tree at Allely Estate Auckland](/images/organic-ground-ceremony-flowers-beneath-a-tree-at-allely-estate-auckla)

We began with a moss-covered base and layers of flowing bear grass. The fine strands of grass created movement, softened the outline of the arrangement and allowed it to blend into the surrounding landscape.

Among the grasses, we placed sculptural anthuriums, delicate white delphiniums, trailing green amaranthus and soft pink Pastini gerberas. The flowers were given plenty of breathing space, allowing each individual shape to be appreciated without the arrangement feeling too full or formal.

![Close-up of anthuriums Pastini gerberas bear grass and delphiniums](/images/close-up-of-anthuriums-pastini-gerberas-bear-grass-and-delphiniums)

From a distance, the flowers looked loose and natural. Up close, the unusual forms and careful placement gave the design its more modern, editorial edge.

![Film photograph of the garden wedding ceremony at Allely Estate](/images/film-photograph-of-the-garden-wedding-ceremony-at-allely-estate)

## Pastini gerberas with a personal meaning

Gerberas hold a special meaning for Claire and Dan, so I wanted them to feel like a thoughtful part of the design rather than simply another flower in the palette. I chose soft pink Pastini gerberas for their fine, curly petals and slightly unusual shape. Used individually throughout the arrangement, they added a delicate and playful detail while still feeling modern and refined.

To complement them, I selected Nursia anthuriums with their soft green-cream base and blush-pink edges. Their sculptural form brought contrast to the finer textures, while their colouring tied the greenery and soft pink tones together beautifully.

![Soft pink Pastini gerberas and Nursia anthuriums in the ceremony arrangement](/images/soft-pink-pastini-gerberas-and-nursia-anthuriums-in-the-ceremony-arran)

## A modern cascading bridal bouquet

Claire’s bridal bouquet followed the same organic and sculptural direction as the ceremony flowers.

It featured Nursia anthuriums, white and pale pink roses and tulips, cascading white phalaenopsis orchids and trailing green amaranthus. Together, they created a softly elongated shape with movement through the front and base of the bouquet.

![Claire holding her cascading orchid and anthurium bridal bouquet](/images/claire-holding-her-cascading-orchid-and-anthurium-bridal-bouquet)

![Close-up of a sculptural bridal bouquet with orchids anthuriums and green amaranthus](/images/close-up-of-a-sculptural-bridal-bouquet-with-orchids-anthuriums-and-gr)

The anthuriums gave the bouquet its sculptural structure, while the orchids brought softness and a sense of flow. The long strands of green amaranthus added another natural, trailing element and gave the bouquet a slightly unexpected silhouette.

The gentle cream, blush and green palette looked beautiful against Claire’s elegant white gown and delicate lace veil. It felt romantic without being overly traditional, and its asymmetrical shape allowed it to make a statement without overwhelming her dress.

![Claire and Dan walking from their Allely Estate garden ceremony](/images/claire-and-dan-walking-from-their-allely-estate-garden-ceremony)

## Soft and simple bridesmaid bouquets

For Claire’s bridesmaids, we created smaller hand-tied bouquets using Nursia anthuriums alongside a selection of soft white and pale pink flowers.

![Bridesmaids holding soft white and pale pink bouquets with dusty blue dresses](/images/bridesmaids-holding-soft-white-and-pale-pink-bouquets-with-dusty-blue)

The bridesmaid bouquets were intentionally kept simpler than Claire’s bouquet. Their clean, airy shapes complemented the dusty blue dresses beautifully and allowed the bridal bouquet to remain the main floral focus.

Each bouquet had a slightly different balance of flowers, giving the group a relaxed and natural look while keeping the overall design cohesive.

![Claire and her bridesmaids with modern white blush and green wedding bouquets](/images/claire-and-her-bridesmaids-with-modern-white-blush-and-green-wedding-b)

## Delicate buttonholes for Dan and his groomsmen

Dan’s buttonhole featured a single white orchid with a small trail of green amaranthus, echoing the orchids and cascading textures in Claire’s bouquet.

For the groomsmen, we kept the buttonholes simple and refined, using delicate white flowers with touches of fresh green foliage. The designs felt cohesive with the rest of the wedding flowers while remaining light and understated against their dark suits.

## Repurposing the ceremony arrangement

One of the most important parts of the floral plan was making sure the main ground arrangement could be enjoyed beyond the ceremony.

After Claire and Dan exchanged their vows, the arrangement was carefully moved indoors and placed in front of their sweetheart table.

Outside beneath the tree, the flowers had felt fresh, airy and connected to the garden. Inside the reception space, the same arrangement took on an entirely different mood.

![Ceremony flowers repurposed in front of the sweetheart table](/images/ceremony-flowers-repurposed-in-front-of-the-sweetheart-table)

Set against the ivory table linen and surrounded by silver candlesticks, warm candlelight and soft draping, the flowers became a romantic focal point for the evening. The anthuriums, gerberas and grasses cast beautiful shadows across the front of the table, adding another layer of texture to the reception setting.

![Candlelit sweetheart table with sculptural flowers at Allely Estate](/images/candlelit-sweetheart-table-with-sculptural-flowers-at-allely-estate)

This kind of repurposing works best when it is considered from the beginning. The arrangement needs to suit both locations, be constructed so it can be moved safely, and still look intentional once it reaches its second setting.

![Film photograph of Claire and Dan at their candlelit sweetheart table](/images/film-photograph-of-claire-and-dan-at-their-candlelit-sweetheart-table)

![Allely Estate wedding reception captured on film](/images/allely-estate-wedding-reception-captured-on-film)

For Claire and Dan’s wedding, one floral installation created two very different moments: natural and garden-like for the ceremony, then warm and editorial for the reception.

## Creating impact with a considered floral budget

Claire and Dan’s wedding did not need flowers in every area of Allely Estate to feel beautifully styled.

Instead of spreading the floral budget too thinly, we concentrated it on the details that would have the most impact: Claire’s bouquet, the bridal-party flowers and one sculptural statement arrangement that could be used twice.

The result came from thoughtful flower choices, movement and texture rather than the quantity of flowers.

This wedding was such a lovely reminder that a smaller floral scope does not need to feel like a compromise. With careful planning, distinctive floral ingredients and clever repurposing, it is possible to create flowers that feel personal, cohesive and memorable throughout the day.

It was a real pleasure to create these flowers for Claire and Dan’s wedding at Allely Estate.
