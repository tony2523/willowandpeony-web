The question I am asked most often, usually with a slightly nervous laugh, is: how much should we budget for wedding flowers?

I would rather answer it properly than politely, so here it is: the honest version of where the money goes, and how to spend yours well.

## Where the money really goes

When you buy wedding flowers, the stems themselves are only part of the story. You are also paying for design time, which starts weeks before your wedding with palettes, proposals and sourcing. You are paying for the flowers to be conditioned, stripped, hydrated and arranged over two or three days in the studio. And you are paying for the day itself: delivery, setup at the venue, and the quiet pack-down the morning after while you are still celebrating.

At Willow and Peony, every vase and plinth is included, because a surprise hire bill three weeks before a wedding helps nobody, and delivery, setup and pack-down are itemised in your proposal from the start.

## What moves the price up or down

A few things shape a floral budget more than anything else.

**Scale of installation.** A grounded meadow or an arch is a construction project made of flowers. It is also the thing guests photograph most. Bud vases and candles down a long table create enormous atmosphere for far less labour.

**The flowers themselves.** In-season blooms give you more flower for the same spend. Imported or out-of-season stems can double a recipe's cost without doubling its beauty.

**Venue logistics.** A venue with easy access, generous set-up time and one level is simply faster to style than a third-floor room with a small lift and a tight window. This is why I write [venue guides](/venues/): knowing the room changes the plan.

## Where to spend, where to save

If I could mark two lines on every budget, they would be these.

Spend on your bouquet, because it is in almost every photograph you will frame. And spend on one statement moment, a single piece that defines the room, rather than spreading the budget thinly everywhere.

Save by letting your ceremony flowers work twice. Nearly every wedding we style moves its ceremony arrangements into the reception, and I have written about [exactly how that works](/journal/repurposing-ceremony-flowers-reception/). Save too by trusting seasonality, and by choosing a palette rather than a shopping list of specific stems, so your florist can buy what is most beautiful that week.

> A good floral budget is not about how much you spend. It is about making sure every dollar ends up somewhere you will actually see it.
