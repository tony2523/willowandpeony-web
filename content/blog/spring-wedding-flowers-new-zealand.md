Spring is my favourite season in the studio. The first sweet peas arrive smelling like childhood, the ranunculus open a little more every morning, and suddenly every bucket in the cool room looks like a celebration waiting to happen.

If you are getting married between September and November in New Zealand, you are spoilt. Here is what is blooming, and how I love to use it.

## Why in-season flowers are always my first recommendation

When a flower is in season locally, three good things happen at once. It is fresher, because it has not travelled far or waited long. It is more affordable, because there is plenty of it. And it looks right, because flowers naturally suit the light and mood of their own season.

Couples sometimes arrive with a photo of peonies for a September wedding, and part of my job is gentle honesty: peonies in early spring have crossed an ocean to be there, and they will cost more and give less. Wait until late November and they are glorious. In September, a bowl of ruffled ranunculus will give you the same romance with better manners.

> Seasonal flowers are not a compromise. They are the reason a wedding feels like it belongs to its day.

## What's in season in New Zealand spring

**September** is the season of promise: sweet peas, anemones, ranunculus, freesias, tulips, daffodils and the first blossom branches. Blossom is my secret weapon early in spring. A few branches above a ceremony table bring height and softness that no imported flower can match.

**October** brings the garden into full voice: garden roses begin, lisianthus returns, delphiniums stretch up in blues and whites, and stock fills a room with clove scent. This is the month for airy, layered designs with real movement.

**November** opens the door to summer: the first peonies, hydrangeas gaining confidence, and dahlias starting their long run. If your heart is set on peonies, this is your window.

If you want the full month-by-month picture, my free [wedding flower calendar](/wedding-flower-calendar/) covers the whole year in New Zealand.

## How I design with spring flowers

Spring stems are softer than summer ones, so I design with that in mind: looser shapes, visible air between blooms, and arrangements that look gathered rather than built. Think a bouquet that moves when you walk, aisle meadows that look grown rather than placed, and bud vases that let a single ranunculus be the whole story.

Palettes I love for New Zealand spring: white and green with blossom for classic romance, soft apricot with cream for warmth, and blue delphinium with white for garden weddings like the ones we style at [The Brigham](/venues/the-brigham/).

## A note on weather

Spring in Auckland has opinions. I always design ceremony pieces that can move indoors gracefully, and I build bouquets that hold their composure in wind. It is part of the craft, and a good florist plans for it without being asked.
